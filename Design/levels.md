# Levels

One section per level. Story context is in `story.md`, systems in
`mechanics.md`.

Only the cold open, the tutorial and Floor 10 are specced. Everything
below Floor 10 is still open — see the bottom of this file.

---

## Cold Open — The Party

**Playable, ~4 minutes.** A rented function room. Cheap decorations,
festive music mixed too quiet. The player can wander and talk to a
handful of coworkers; none of it matters, and it shouldn't.

**Beat 1 — The award.** His name is called. Fifteen years. The plaque
is misspelled and the manager reads his job title off a card and gets
that wrong too. Polite applause. He sits back down.

**Beat 2 — The new hire.** Twenty-three, three weeks in, stuck at his
table. Describes the job as temporary — *"Couple years, save up, then
I'll actually do the thing."* No dialogue option exists to warn them.

**Beat 3 — The call.** His phone buzzes mid-conversation. **Mom.** The
prompt to answer requires standing up and leaving a conversation while
people are looking. Most players will let it ring.

If answered: she says nothing important. It changes nothing. It changes
everything.

**Hard cut.** Title card — three days. She died that night.

## Tutorial — The Apartment

**7:12 AM. ~20 minutes.** Small Japanese apartment. Lived-in but not
cared for; somewhere a person sleeps between shifts.

**Objective:** print the letter, get dressed, leave.

### What's in the room

Everything here is optional. The examine system gets taught by being
worth using.

| Object | What it says |
|---|---|
| The plaque | Still in the gift bag on the counter. Never unwrapped past the first look. |
| A black suit | Laid out on the bed, pressed. For today. |
| The abandoned thing | Camera / guitar case / knife roll, dusty, in a closet. Pick one and commit to it. |
| Wall calendar | Work dates, and one entry in her handwriting from a visit years ago. |
| The fridge | Condiments and takeout containers. Nothing that requires cooking. |
| The answering machine | Three unplayed messages. |

### The answering machine

Optional, unmarked, and the emotional core of the tutorial.

- **Five weeks ago.** Chatty, long. Asks about work. Asks when he's
  coming.
- **Two weeks ago.** Shorter. *"I know you're busy. It's alright."*
- **Friday night — the party.** Eleven seconds. *"It's me. Nothing
  important. Call when you can."*

No music sting. The machine beeps into silence afterward.

### The printer puzzle

Teaches interact, examine, and that the game expects the player to
solve things.

1. The computer — the letter is open. Print.
2. The printer does nothing. Red light.
3. No paper. A ream is findable in a closet or under unopened mail.
4. Load it. Still jammed.
5. Open the tray, examine inside, pull out the crumpled sheet — **it's
   a resignation letter dated four years ago.** Same first line.
   Printed, jammed, forgotten, never sent. The game does not comment.
6. Print. Take the letter, keys, badge, suit.

**Out the door, 7:40 AM.** The drive establishes where today is going:
the funeral is at 6:00, four hours away. He has all day. He's going in
to quit first because he is not coming back.

No timer — see `mechanics.md`. The urgency is narrative, and the game
never asks the player to wait.

## Floor 10 — Your Desk

**The floor is built once and played twice.** Identical geometry, two
completely different games. Best value-per-asset in the project: the
player already knows the space, so the second pass is re-read as a
puzzle.

### Part A — Normal (8:50 AM, ~15 minutes)

Badge in at the lobby turnstile. It works. **The reader is on the
outside only** — the player won't clock it, and that's the whole
ending.

Elevator to 10. Open-plan floor: cubicles, break room, copy room,
meeting rooms along the window wall, Halloran's office in the corner.
Keyboards, HVAC, a printer somewhere, one phone ringing that nobody
answers.

**Objective: give the letter to Halloran.** He isn't in yet, so the
player wanders.

#### People

- **Denise** — 22 years here. Warm, genuinely fine, no regrets she'll
  admit to. *"You'll be back. Everyone comes back."* She's the version
  of him that stopped fighting, and she isn't unhappy, which is worse.
- **The new hire from the party** — now has a desk. Doesn't remember
  meeting him. Repeats the "couple years" line almost verbatim.
- **His desk** — someone else's coat on the chair, someone else's mug.
  Hot-desked while he was out making funeral arrangements. His things
  are boxed underneath. Nobody did this to be cruel.

Examining the box gives the character: a fifteen-year-old photo, a dead
succulent, a birthday card signed by people who've since left — and
his badge, which he is still carrying.

#### The resignation

Halloran arrives. Knock, hand it over. He reads it, isn't surprised,
isn't angry, isn't moved, and signs it.

> *"Fifteen years. That's a long time. Good for you."*

Ninety seconds. Done. He goes back to his monitor.

The player gets real, earned relief. The game appears to be over and
it's 9:05 AM.

### Part B — After (9:05 AM, ~20 minutes)

Nothing visually changes. The floor is identical. The horror is
entirely mechanical.

He walks to the elevator, badges the reader, and gets a red light.

#### The four things the player tries, in order

1. **The elevator** — needs an active badge to select a floor. Dead.
2. **Halloran's office** — he's gone, a recurring 9:15 owns his
   morning. His calendar is visible through the glass; first hint that
   schedules are information.
3. **The stairwell** — badge-locked. Everything is badge-locked. It
   always was.
4. **Asking someone** — Denise is sympathetic and useless. *"Call IT.
   Floor 7."* The desk phone routes to an automated line that tells him
   to file a ticket on the intranet, which needs the login that died at
   9:05. First taste of the circle that Floor 9 closes.

#### The three lessons

**People are doors.** Someone walks to the elevator and badges it. The
player gets in with them. Nobody objects; nobody looks. They've just
used a person as a key, and the game never told them to.

**The official channel is a dead end.** Every helpful person points at
a process, and every process needs the access he no longer has. Nobody
is obstructing him. It just doesn't work.

**Credentials exist.** In the copy room, a sticky note on a monitor
bezel: `dokafor / Mochi14`. It isn't needed yet. They'll remember it.

#### The last beat

He rides down with a coworker who gets out at 9. He stays in.

The elevator won't go to the lobby — that's an active-badge floor. It
will go down to 9, 8, 7. IT is on 7.

**The doors close and the panel offers nothing above his current
floor.** That's the game announcing its rules.

### Teaching checklist

| Mechanic | Taught by |
|---|---|
| Badge readers gate everything | Elevator, stairwell |
| His badge is dead | Part B open |
| Follow people through doors | The elevator ride |
| Schedules are information | Halloran's calendar |
| Credentials are findable | Copy room sticky note |
| Official channels are circular | The IT phone line |
| One-way descent | Final beat |

## Floor 9 — Sales

**~25 minutes.** The first floor that has to work on gameplay alone,
with no story rug-pull holding it up.

**Objective: find another way down.**

### The dead end that starts it

He arrives on 9 by accident — rode down with a coworker who got out
here, and the panel stopped responding once he was alone. No active
badge, no floor selection.

Every official route closes in about ninety seconds:

- **Elevator** — needs an active badge.
- **Stairwell** — badge-locked, and on 9 it only goes *up*. Roof
  access. There's a sign. One-way descent stated by the architecture.
- **IT is on 7** — which he can't reach.
- **The desk phone** routes to an automated line: *file a ticket on the
  intranet.* The intranet needs his login. His login was deactivated at
  9:05.

**You need a badge to file the ticket that gets you a badge.**

That's the joke, and it's the engine. Once the player hits it they stop
looking for official permission for the rest of the game.

### The floor

Sales. Loud, performatively cheerful, and he is walking through it in a
black suit.

Bullpen of desks, leaderboard wall, glass conference room, break room,
copy room, Balan's office, elevator lobby. They ring a bell for closed
deals — establish that sound here so it can ring on an empty floor
later.

**The service door** is at the back of the floor. The player finds it
by watching a delivery cart go through it. Nobody badges it; it's
back-of-house, and nobody secures back-of-house.

### The service corridor

Complete change of visual language: bare concrete, exposed conduit, no
carpet, harsh unshaded fluorescents, the floor's HVAC roaring. This is
the vocabulary for "off the official path" and it recurs all the way
down.

At the end: **the freight elevator. Dead.** No panel lights.

### The breaker panel

A utility closet off the corridor. **Twelve breakers, unlabeled.**

The puzzle loop is: flip one, walk back out onto the sales floor, see
what changed, come back. It forces the player to re-read the space
repeatedly, and the feedback is instant and physical.

| Breaker | What it does |
|---|---|
| Bullpen lights, zone A | Goes dark. Complaints. Someone stands up. |
| Bullpen lights, zone B | Same, other half |
| Vending machines | Break room goes quiet and dark |
| Copy room | The big multifunction printers die |
| PA / floor music | The cheerful playlist stops |
| Leaderboard display | Goes black. **Nobody notices.** |
| HVAC | See below |
| Conference room screen | Mid-presentation |
| Dead circuit | Nothing. Wasted flip. |
| Freight elevator | The one you want |

A **maintenance clipboard** in the closet narrows twelve down to about
three for players who examine it. It doesn't gate progress — brute
force still works, it just takes longer.

### The two-stage catch

Flip the freight breaker and **it trips straight back off.** Something
else is overloading the shared feed.

Scrawled on the clipboard in someone's handwriting:

> `FREIGHT + COPY ON SAME FEED — DO NOT RUN BOTH`

Kill the copy room, then the freight elevator holds. Load balancing —
simple, logical, satisfying, and it makes the clipboard worth reading
without making it mandatory.

### The optional terminal

Marcus Vance's desk is empty. Nameplate gives `mvance`. The boat photo
reads **SECOND WIND**; the plaque says **TOP CLOSER — Q3 2021**.
Password: `SecondWind2021`. Decoys on the desk — protein shaker, golf
scorecard, "WORLD'S OKAYEST GOLFER" mug.

**This is not on the critical path.** It's the floor's reward for
paying attention, and it establishes the rule the rest of the game runs
on: *passwords are two details from the desk.*

What it gives: the shared-feed information as a second route to the
same clue, and an email from a department that isn't on the directory,
with a headcount spreadsheet attached whose columns aren't sales
figures.

### Horror beats

**Kill the HVAC.** The sound floor of the entire game — the hum that's
been under every scene since the apartment — stops. And everybody keeps
typing. Nobody looks up. It costs nothing to build and it's the best
moment on the floor.

**The glass conference room.** Forty people, motionless, all facing the
same direction, and no sound comes through the glass — not muffled,
nothing. It holds as long as the player looks. Then it ends and all
forty stand at once. Completely deniable. It's just a meeting.

**Balan's office.** A second leaderboard, same layout as the wall
outside, different columns: `HOURS LOGGED` · `RESPONSE LATENCY` ·
`RETENTION RISK` · `PROJECTED YIELD`. No explanation, no document dump.
Just a board ranking people by things a sales floor has no reason to
measure.

### Exit

Freight elevator down to 8.

## Routes Down — the level vocabulary

There are only so many ways through a floor, and each one is a level.
**One route per floor, never repeated.** That's eight distinct levels
and nobody needs a story reason to want to go downstairs.

The puzzle on each floor is the same shape with different content:
**find the route, then make it usable** — power it, clear it, open it,
reach it.

| Route | Status |
|---|---|
| Elevator, riding down with someone | Used — Floor 10 |
| Freight elevator + breaker panel | Used — Floor 9 |
| **Renovation zone / ceiling drop** | Unused. Plastic sheeting, half the ceiling gone, a gap that drops into the floor below. The level is getting high enough to reach it. Cheap to build. |
| **Elevator shaft + fire axe** | Unused. The only tool in an office is behind fire-cabinet glass — fifteen years of following rules and the first one he breaks is smashing it with his elbow. Save it for lower down, where it hits harder. |
| **Atrium / void** | Unused. The floor opens onto open air. Scaffolding, catwalk, window-washer rig. |
| **Fire escape** | Unused. Out a window, down the outside, back in one floor lower. |
| **Chute** | Unused. Mail, laundry, garbage. Nasty and memorable. Use once. |
